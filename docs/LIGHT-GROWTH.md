# Light and growth: sources

Filled 28 November 2026 by the default method (CLAUDE.md): a value for each
species written from knowledge first, then checked against NC State's Plant
Toolbox and the Morton Arboretum, which both give light and growth rate in
labeled fields, and revised.

The 46 taxa those two sites do not cover (hybrids, cultivars and less common
trees) were filled the same day from other sources: Utah State's Tree Browser
and Varga Arboretum pages, Cornell's Woody Plants Database, the RHS, Oregon
State, NDSU, the University of Minnesota, Chicago Botanic Garden, Purdue
Arboretum, Boone County Arboretum, Clemson and other extension services, the
Forest Service's Silvics, and, for named cultivars with nothing else, breeder,
patent and nursery pages (allowed by CLAUDE.md). Each row below names its
sources.

## How disagreements were settled

- **Light.** NC State and Morton list every light level a plant grows in, so
  they often say sun to part shade for trees that do best in full sun. Where
  the Forest Service's Silvics or FEIS classes a tree as intolerant of shade,
  it is `full sun` (bitternut and mockernut hickory, honeylocust, sweetgum,
  sassafras, black locust, the aspens, black willow, several oaks and pines).
  Otherwise the sources' range stands, even where it differed from the first
  draft (tree-of-heaven, for one, is moderately shade tolerant).
- **Growth.** Where the sources and the draft differ by one step, the value
  spans both (`slow to medium`, `medium to fast`). Where they differ by two
  steps, more sources were sought until most agreed: bitternut hickory is
  `medium` from Silvics' growth table (about 40 ft at 30 years), and devil's
  walkingstick `slow to medium` (its trunks grow slowly; only its root suckers
  are fast).
- **Cultivars** take their species' values unless a source describes the
  cultivar itself. Amber Glow dawn redwood, Snow Fountains cherry, Emerald
  arborvitae and Camperdown elm now have growth from sources on the cultivar.
  Amber Glow's light is `sun to part shade`, as its breeder gives it, though
  the species wants full sun.

## Blank by design

Genus-only entries ("Ash, Fraxinus sp.") and "Unknown" have no light or
growth: there is no one species to describe.
