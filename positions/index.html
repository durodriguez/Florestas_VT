How it works
  • Drag any dot onto the crown it belongs to. No need to select it first. On a phone or tablet, touch and hold a dot for half a second until a white ring appears, then slide. A quick tap selects the tree; a finger that moves straight away pans the map.
  • With a tree selected, the arrow keys nudge it 0.2 m (1 m with Shift).
  • Shift+click or Ctrl+click (⌘) adds a tree to the selection, or takes it out. Rectangle or Circle, then drag on the map, selects every tree inside (hold Shift or Ctrl to add to the selection). The shape stays: drag its square handles to resize it, the round one to move it, and the selection follows.
  • Mark as not there, or the Delete key, for trees with nothing on the imagery where the record puts them. They turn dark with a dashed red ring and can be restored until you export.
  • ◎ (top right) shows where you are, for checking positions on site: a blue dot that follows you, in a shaded circle as wide as the device's own accuracy estimate. Phone GPS is often off by 3–10 m, so judge a tree against the circle, not the dot. Tap ◎ again to stop.
  • On a phone, drag the bar between this panel and the map up or down to give either more room. Double-tap it to put it back.
  • Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) undoes the last change. Esc deselects.
  • Moved trees turn gold, with a line back to where the record had them.
  • Every change is kept in this browser as you go. When you are done, Export changes and send the file — npm run positions applies it. A move is not a survey visit, and the public map will not show it as one; a tree marked not there gets a not found record and leaves the map, its history kept.
LiDAR crown peaks 0

Tops of tree crowns found in Vermont's statewide LiDAR, flown 21 April 2023 (leaf-off), across the whole campus boundary. A peak shows that a tree stood there, not which tree. Trees under about 3 m, crowns merged in a clump, and trees overhanging buildings give no peak. The layers button turns them off. Details: docs/LIDAR.md.

Tree crowns (SAL) 0

The Spatial Analysis Lab's LiDAR tree centroids, 2023: each crown's center and its radius, drawn as a circle. Load the .zip as SAL sent it (or its .shp, .dbf and .prj together). Kept in this browser only — never uploaded or published. Only crowns on or near campus are kept.
